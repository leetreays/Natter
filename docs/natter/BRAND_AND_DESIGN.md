# Natter — Brand & Design

## Brand idea

Natter should feel like a trusted, hopeful journey into digital friendship.

Core expression:

**Connect · Protect · Grow**

## Logo direction

Preferred current direction:

- retain the original shield concept as the primary identity;
- shield should feel protective but not militaristic;
- capital N and shield should each remain recognisable;
- white outline should be refined/thin rather than heavy;
- diagonal of the N should feel visually balanced with its vertical strokes;
- symbolism should be subtle rather than overloaded.

A three-dot motif has been explored.

The dots can quietly represent the three Natter promises / values:

- Connect
- Protect
- Grow

Known colour-order exploration:

- blue
- pink
- green

The third dot was specifically requested in green in one iteration.

## Core colours

Known application palette:

- Natter Blue — `#3DA6F3`
- Natter Green — `#A4D35A`
- Natter Yellow — `#FBC02D`
- Natter Pink — `#FF5DA2`
- Natter Navy — `#06112E`

Use current asset/code definitions where they differ.

## Parent UI direction

Known `ParentBrandScaffold` direction:

- deep navy;
- dark blue cards;
- transparent app bar;
- rounded cards;
- subtle borders;
- premium/calm feel.

## Website visual narrative

The website concept moves through a dusk-to-dawn journey.

Darkness gradually gives way to light as the user discovers Natter.

### Constant elements

- top header/banner;
- shield/logo;
- three navigation links;
- central progression cue / arrow;
- sparks/orbs as subtle narrative elements.

### Header labels

Agreed:

- Story
- How it works
- Join the journey

## Orbs / sparks

Three golden orbs initially appear in a triangular arrangement.

They gradually take on Natter colours as the story progresses.

Known direction:

- early scenes: all gold;
- Connect scene: one becomes blue;
- other two remain gold;
- later progression introduces remaining product colours.

Keep them subtle.

## Website typography

Direction:

- soft;
- rounded;
- spacious;
- minimal copy;
- strong titles where needed;
- restrained emphasis elsewhere.

## Design rule

Natter should look safe and magical enough for children, but credible and sophisticated enough for parents, schools and future partners.
