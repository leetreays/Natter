# Natter — Product Language

Use established Natter language unless a deliberate naming decision changes it.

## Core brand language

- **Natter**
- **Connect · Protect · Grow**
- **Digital Rite of Passage**
- **Friendship Journey**
- **Friendship Health**
- **Repair Momentum**
- **Meaningful Moments**
- **Outcome Engine**
- **Kindness Engine**
- **Protected Delivery**
- **Quiet Hours**
- **Digital Readiness Report**
- **Daily Sparks**
- **Privacy Promise**

## Canonical principle language

- "Children own their conversations."
- "Parents understand relationships, not messages."
- "Celebrate growth more than monitor mistakes."
- "Privacy is a feature, not a compromise."
- "Built through kindness, repair and trust."
- "Your child's conversations remain private."

## Tone

Natter should feel:

- warm;
- safe;
- thoughtful;
- hopeful;
- age-appropriate;
- non-judgemental;
- quietly intelligent;
- trustworthy without feeling institutional.

Avoid language that makes the child feel clinically analysed, scored, accused or watched.

## Parent language

Prefer relationship-level observations:

- "This friendship has shown signs of repair."
- "There have been a few moments where support may help."
- "They appear to be reconnecting."

Avoid unnecessary message-content language or exposing private wording.

## Child language

Prefer coaching that is brief, understandable and actionable.

Good direction:

- invite reconsideration;
- explain effect;
- offer a rewrite;
- support repair;
- avoid lectures.

## Brand concepts

The three-dot idea has been explored as a subtle representation of three core values/promises, aligned with:

- Connect
- Protect
- Grow

The shield/N identity should retain subtle meaning rather than becoming overloaded with obvious symbols.
