# Natter — Product Vision

## One-line definition

Natter is a child/parent messaging and friendship-learning platform that helps children learn to build healthy relationships while giving parents understanding without surveillance.

## Why Natter exists

Children increasingly learn friendship, conflict, belonging and communication through digital spaces. Traditional safety products tend to frame this as a monitoring problem. Natter takes a different position:

**The goal is not merely to stop bad messages. The goal is to help a child become a better communicator and friend.**

Natter therefore combines private child communication, age-appropriate coaching, behavioural intelligence and parent-facing relationship insights.

## Core promise

### Connect
Help children belong, make friends and begin conversations.

### Protect
Help children feel safe, parents feel reassured and trusted adults support without intruding into private conversations.

### Grow
Help children leave the experience as more capable, thoughtful and resilient communicators.

Canonical expression:

**Connect · Protect · Grow**

## The central product tension

Natter must do two things at once:

- protect children;
- preserve meaningful privacy and autonomy.

The product should not resolve that tension by simply giving adults access to everything.

The intended model is:

> Children own their conversations. Parents understand relationships, not messages.

## What success looks like

Success is not maximising screen time or indefinite retention.

A successful child should gradually develop the confidence and communication maturity to need less intervention and, ultimately, to outgrow Natter.

This is embodied in the **Digital Rite of Passage**: a platform-level graduation/end-state that recognises readiness and growth.

It is not a badge awarded per individual friendship.

## Distinctive product ideas

Natter's product identity includes:

- private child messaging;
- friendship-building rather than social-media performance;
- coaching before punishment where appropriate;
- Protected Delivery;
- Quiet Hours;
- behavioural signals that inform support;
- parent insights that describe relationships without exposing message content;
- Friendship Health;
- Repair Momentum;
- Meaningful Moments;
- Outcome Engine;
- Friendship Journey;
- Digital Readiness Report;
- Daily Sparks / quests;
- Digital Rite of Passage.

## Long-term ambition

Natter should become a trusted first meaningful digital friendship environment: a place where a child can practise communication, recover from mistakes, understand the effect of their words and gradually become ready for wider digital independence.
