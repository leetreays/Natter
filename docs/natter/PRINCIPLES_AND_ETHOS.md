# Natter — Principles & Ethos

This document is a product-level source of truth.

Implementation may evolve. These principles should not be changed merely to simplify engineering.

## Five guiding principles

### 1. Children own their conversations

A child's private conversation should remain theirs.

Safety systems may analyse behaviour and intervene, but Natter is not designed as a parent message-reading product.

### 2. Parents understand relationships, not messages

Parents should receive useful context such as patterns, relationship health, moments of repair and areas where support may help.

They should not be given a transcript-like window into a child's private conversations.

### 3. Celebrate growth more than monitor mistakes

Natter should notice kindness, repair, reconnection and maturity — not only problematic behaviour.

A child should feel that the system believes improvement is possible.

### 4. Privacy is a feature, not a compromise

Privacy is part of the trust proposition.

The product should seek the minimum visibility needed for meaningful safeguarding and support rather than assuming more visibility is always better.

### 5. Behavioural intelligence powers experiences, not visible scores

Internal behavioural signals can drive coaching, relationship understanding, parent insights and safety decisions.

Those signals should not become simplistic public/child-facing scores that label a child as "good", "bad", "toxic", "risky" or similar.

## Decision filters

When evaluating a feature, ask:

1. Does this help a child become a better communicator or friend?
2. Does this protect without unnecessarily surveilling?
3. Does this preserve the child's dignity and agency?
4. Does it give a parent understanding rather than raw access?
5. Does it reward progress and repair, not just detect failure?
6. Could the same safety outcome be achieved with less data exposure?
7. Does it move the child toward eventual independence?

## Coaching philosophy

Where risk allows, Natter should favour:

**notice → coach → rewrite/reconsider → repair → learn**

rather than:

**detect → punish → shame**

Some behaviour may still require blocking or stronger intervention. The coaching-first philosophy does not remove safeguarding boundaries.

## Relationship philosophy

Friendships are dynamic.

Natter should avoid permanently labelling a friendship based on one bad interaction. The system should account for:

- patterns;
- escalation;
- repair;
- time;
- repeated targeting;
- moments of kindness;
- reconnection;
- resilience.

## Parent trust

Parent trust should come from clear boundaries and meaningful insight, not from hidden surveillance.

The parent experience should be able to say, credibly:

> Your child's conversations remain private.

and:

> You understand relationships, not messages.

## Graduation

Natter is designed to be outgrown.

The Digital Rite of Passage is a trust-building feature precisely because the product does not claim the child should remain dependent on Natter forever.
